# 5.2 The Circulatory System
**15-07-2018**

- Circulatory system is a means by which blood moves throughout your body.

## The Circulatory System Functions:
- Carries nutrients towards cells.
- Remove wastes from cells in one part of the body to different target tissues.
- Distribute heat throughout the body along with the kidney.
- Maintain levels of body fluid.
- Provides Oxygen for cellular respiration.
- Transport immune cells throughout the body to defend against invading organisms.

## The Heart Walls
The heart walls consist of three layers:

1.  **Endocardium:** is the thin membrane that lines the interior of the heart, which contains coronary arteries that supply blood for the heart muscle.
2.  **Myocardium:** The middle layer of the heart. It is the heart muscle and it is the thickest layer of the heart.
3.  **Epicardium:** is the thin layer on the surface of the heart in which coronary arteries lie.

**Pericardium:** is the thin sac that the heart sits in, often filled with a small amount of fluid which separates the heart from the other structures in the chest cavity such as the lungs.

## Heart Valves
**Q: What types of valves are there between the heart chambers?**

Valves are structures inside the heart that prevent the backflow of blood. There are four types of valves:

1.  **Tricuspid valve (Right atrioventricular valve):** Separates the right atrium from the right ventricle.
2.  **Bicuspid valve (Left atrioventricular valve):** Separates the left atrium from the left ventricle.
3.  **Aortic Valve (Aortic Semilunar Valve):** Found between the left ventricle and Aorta. It ensures that oxygenated blood does not flow back into the left ventricle.
4.  **Pulmonary Valve:** Separates the right ventricle from the pulmonary arteries, which transport deoxygenated blood into the lungs. It prevents the backflow of blood into the right ventricle from pulmonary arteries.

# Types of Circulation
**17-07-2018**

**Q1. What are the two types of circulation?**

### A. Pulmonary Circulation
- The movement of blood from heart to lungs for oxygenation then back to the heart again.
- Deoxygenated blood from the body leaves the systemic circulation when it enters into the right atrium through the superior and inferior vena cava.
- The blood is then pumped through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle.
- From the right ventricle, blood is pumped through the pulmonary valve and into the pulmonary artery.
- The pulmonary artery splits into the right and left pulmonary arteries and the blood in the arteries travels into each lung.

### B. Systematic Circulation
- It is the circulation of blood between the heart and different parts of the body.
- After blood reaches the lungs it becomes oxygenated and pulmonary veins transport the oxygenated blood from the lungs towards the left atrium.
- From the left atrium, blood is transported into the left ventricle through the bicuspid valve.
- Oxygenated blood from the left ventricle moves towards the Aorta through the aortic valve and is transported towards different parts of the body.

### Double Circulation Diagram
- **Right Atrium ($CO_2$)** $\rightarrow$ Tricuspid valve $\rightarrow$ **Right Ventricle ($CO_2$)** $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary valve $\rightarrow$ **Lungs**
- **Lungs ($O_2$)** $\rightarrow$ Pulmonary vein $\rightarrow$ **Left Atrium ($O_2$)** $\rightarrow$ Bicuspid valve $\rightarrow$ **Left Ventricle ($O_2$)** $\rightarrow$ Aortic valve $\rightarrow$ **Body**

# Cardiac Cycle
**22-07-2018**

- The period of time that begins with the contraction of atria and ends with ventricular relaxation is known as the cardiac cycle.
- The period of contraction that the heart undergoes while it pumps blood into the circulation is called **systole**.
- The period of relaxation that occurs as the heart chambers fill with blood is called **diastole**.

## The Heart Sound
- In a normal healthy heart there are only two audible heart sounds (**lub** and **dup (dub)**).
- **Lub** or first heart sound is created by the closing of the atrioventricular valves during ventricular contraction.
- The second heart sound **Dup** or **Dub** is the sound of closing of semilunar valves during ventricular diastole.
- **Stethoscope:** an instrument used to listen to heart sounds.
- **Murmur:** unusual sound coming from the heart due to turbulent flow of blood.

# Blood Pressure
- Blood pressure is the force of blood on the walls of the arteries.
- **Sphygmomanometer:** an instrument that measures blood pressure.
- **Systolic pressure:** Occurs when the heart muscle contracts and pumps blood.
- **Diastolic pressure:** Occurs when the heart muscle relaxes and fills with blood.

## Types of Systemic Circulation
The systemic circulation is divided into two:

1.  **Coronary Circulation:**
    - Carries blood to and from the cardiac muscle of the heart itself.
    - **Coronary artery:** carries oxygenated blood to the heart muscle.
    - **Coronary veins:** carries deoxygenated blood from the heart muscle.
2.  **Portal Circulation:**
    - Is the circulation of blood between the heart and the liver.
    - **Hepatic artery:** supplies oxygenated blood to the liver.
    - **Hepatic vein:** carries deoxygenated blood away from the liver to the heart.
    - **Hepatic portal vein:** carries digestive end products of food absorbed from the gut to the liver.

# Blood Vessels
**28-07-2018**

- They are the pathway or the pipe through which transportation of blood takes place.
- There are three main types of blood vessels:

### i. Artery
- Is a blood vessel that is used to carry blood away from the heart.
- Arteries carry oxygenated blood except for the pulmonary artery and umbilical artery.
- **Pulmonary artery:** carries deoxygenated blood from the heart into the lungs.
- **Umbilical artery:** carries deoxygenated blood from the fetus towards the placenta.
- Arteries have relatively thick and elastic walls and contain no valves.

### ii. Vein
- Is a blood vessel that is used to return blood back to the heart.
- Veins carry deoxygenated blood except for the pulmonary vein and umbilical vein.
- **Pulmonary vein:** carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart.
- **Umbilical vein:** carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus.
- Veins have relatively thin and less elastic walls and contain valves.

### iii. Capillaries
- They are single-celled thin-walled vessels.
- They connect arteries with veins.
- They carry oxygenated blood to cells and deoxygenated blood from cells.
- Capillaries have no valves.

## The Main Blood Vessels of the Body and Their Function:
1.  **Renal artery:** supplies oxygenated blood to the kidneys.
2.  **Iliac artery:** supplies oxygenated blood to the lower parts of the body and legs.
3.  **Mesenteric artery:** supplies oxygenated blood to the gut.
4.  **Carotid artery:** supplies oxygenated blood to the head and neck.
5.  **Jugular vein:** carries deoxygenated blood from the head.
6.  **Renal vein:** carries deoxygenated blood from the kidneys.
7.  **Iliac vein:** carries deoxygenated blood from the legs and lower body.

# Blood
**29-07-2018**

- Blood is a constantly circulating fluid providing the body with nutrition, Oxygen, and waste removal.
- The liquid portion of the blood is called **plasma**.
- Plasma contains proteins that help blood to clot and transport substances through the blood.
- Plasma also contains glucose and other dissolved nutrients.
- The solid portion of blood consists of **blood cells**.

## Blood Cells
There are three types of blood cells:

### A. Red Blood Cells (Erythrocytes)
- They are biconcave, disc-shaped, relatively larger microscopic non-nucleated cells.
- RBCs normally make up 40-50% of the total blood volume.
- They contain a red iron-containing protein called **haemoglobin**, which is an oxygen-carrying and transporting molecule that makes up 95% of the red blood cell.
- The red color of blood is primarily due to oxygenated red blood cells called **oxyhaemoglobin**.
- $\text{haemoglobin} + O_2 \rightarrow \text{Oxyhaemoglobin}$
- Carbon dioxide is also carried in the blood mainly as hydrogen carbonate.

#### Red Blood Cells Adaptation:
- Haemoglobin is largely responsible for efficient Oxygen transportation.
- The biconcave disc-shape of RBCs provides a large surface area for the diffusion of Oxygen into and out of the cell.
- Lack of nucleus in mature RBCs means that the maximum amount of space is available to pack in haemoglobin molecules to carry Oxygen.
- Very thin cell surface membrane enables $O_2$ and $CO_2$ to diffuse in and out rapidly.
- Red blood cells are manufactured in the bone marrow of short bones.
- RBCs have a limited life span of about 120 days.

### B. White Blood Cells (Leukocytes)
- WBCs exist in variable numbers and types.
- They make up a very small part of blood's volume (around 1% in a healthy person).
- They are not limited to blood but also occur in the body in:
    - the spleen
    - the lymph glands
    - the liver
    - the bone marrow
- They are mostly manufactured in the white bone marrow of long bones.
- Other lymphocytes are manufactured in the lymph glands (at the base of the neck) and in the spleen.
- They are irregularly shaped, larger than erythrocytes, have a nucleus, and are colorless.
- They defend the body against pathogenic diseases.
- Leukocytes are classified as:
    - **Phagocytes:** groups of white blood cells that engulf or ingest infectious pathogenic microorganisms (e.g., Granulocytes, Macrophages).
    - **Lymphocytes:** groups of WBCs which help to produce antibodies.

### C. Platelets (Thrombocytes)
- They have an essential role in blood clotting.
- They are very small.
- They don't have a nucleus.
- Blood clotting has two important functions:
    1. To stop bleeding during injury.
    2. To prevent disease-causing pathogenic microorganisms from getting into the body.
- Platelets release an enzyme which sets off a chemical reaction in the body.
- The result of the reaction changes **fibrinogen** in the blood plasma (a plasma protein) into **fibrin threads** (insoluble fibrin network).
- This insoluble fibrin thread plugs the injured (wound) part and results in the clotting of the blood (stops bleeding).
